# Competitive research and source register

Status: **proposal input, not a decision.** Written 2026-11-03 in the `Dojo-main` checkout (branch `test/local-main-e2e`, base `origin/main` `56af7a4`). Companion documents: [`PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md`](PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md), [`FUTURE_ARCHITECTURE.md`](FUTURE_ARCHITECTURE.md), [`ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md).

## Executive summary

**What the research shows.**

1. **Established practice platforms are built on three pillars:** a large problem bank with a judge, scheduled public contests with ratings, and credentials or hiring links [R:S01, S04, S09 to S12]. Each pillar is costly to build and already well served. This is why `PRODUCT_STRATEGY.md` does not make code practice or contests the first move.
2. **Structured-learning products win on bounded paths:** named topics, fixed-size lists, explicit prerequisites and visible completion [R:S13 to S15]. This pattern suits Dojo's five skills.
3. **Duolingo's engagement system is a set of linked mechanics around one core lesson loop:** streaks with a built-in freeze, weekly leagues with opt-out, paired weekly quests with nudges, onboarding that captures goals, and adaptive difficulty and spaced repetition underneath [R:S16 to S23]. The company also reports its own effect sizes, which are correlations or company-run tests, not independent evidence [R:S16, S19, S24].
4. **Behavioural science supports a gentler design than the most aggressive retention tactics:** motivation, ability and a prompt must converge [R:S26]; autonomy, competence and relatedness support persistence [R:S27]; and manipulative design is a regulatory concern [R:S29].
5. **No researched product teaches or ranks agent-workflow quality.** That is Dojo's open ground and its main product risk, and nothing in this research can validate it.

**Recommended next milestone** is defined in [`ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md) section 6 (M1, "Make the loop real"). For this document the follow-up is small: verify the **[K]** items and read the goal-gradient paper before either is cited as a finding.

## Method and limits

- **Pass:** a web search per product family on 2026-11-03, restricted to first-party domains where possible, then a full page fetch where a claim needed it.
- **Read depth.** Most sources below were read as **search excerpts**, which contain the passage quoted but not the whole page. One page (S19) was read in full. The register states the depth for each source. A claim is only as strong as that depth.
- **Not researched:** private product internals, paywalled content, A/B test results that vendors do not publish, and anything behind a login. Features that exist in the products but were not confirmed from a first-party page are marked **[K]**.
- **Duolingo** is studied for its _mechanisms_. Its published numbers are the company's own and are cited as such. Correlations it reports (for example "learners who follow friends are 5.6x more likely to finish a course") are **not** evidence that the mechanism _causes_ the outcome.
- **A pattern working for a language app does not mean it works for developers.** Every Duolingo-derived idea below is a **[H]** until Dojo measures it.

### Duolingo: verified mechanics

| Mechanism                         | What the first-party source says                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Source   |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| Streaks                           | The streak is described as designed around habit-building and daily use. Duolingo reports that adding streak animations raised the likelihood that a brand-new learner was still using the app seven days later by 1.7% (the company's own figure; the excerpt read does not say whether that is a relative change or percentage points). | S16      |
| Streak freeze                     | A learner equips a freeze in advance; it fills a missed day so the streak continues. Duolingo argues that leeway helps people stay persistent, and now allows two freezes at once.                                                                                                                                                        | S16, S17 |
| Protecting streaks from outages   | Duolingo reuses the freeze logic to repair streaks broken by its own site problems, and says this improved learner experience and reduced support load.                                                                                                                                                                                   | S17      |
| Weekly leagues                    | A new weekly league begins each Sunday in the learner's timezone; you are matched with learners of similar study habits and a similar timezone, across all courses; promotion and demotion follow weekly effort; a tournament exists for the most engaged.                                                                                | S18      |
| League opt-out                    | A learner can opt out by turning off a public-profile setting.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | S18      |
| Abuse monitoring                  | Duolingo states it monitors for irregular XP and removes illegitimate gains from leaderboards.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | S18      |
| Friends Quests                    | Each week a learner is paired at random with a friend who follows them back and has done a lesson that week, assigned a shared challenge to finish in five days, with a reward chest. Learners can send pre-written nudges, and can gift a gem-funded XP boost. Learners can turn the feature off.                                        | S19      |
| Friends and completion (reported) | Duolingo reports that learners who follow friends are 5.6 times more likely to finish a course. This is a correlation the company reports; it is **not** shown to be causal.                                                                                                                                                              | S19      |
| Onboarding                        | The first run asks about motivations and goals and offers to turn on notifications to establish a study habit.                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | S20      |
| Adaptive difficulty               | A proprietary model (Birdbrain) estimates what each learner knows and how hard each exercise is, and tailors lessons accordingly.                                                                                                                                                                                                         | S21, S25 |
| Spaced repetition                 | Half-life regression, a trainable model that schedules review from a forgetting curve, grounded in the spacing effect.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | S22, S23 |
| Engagement metrics                | Duolingo reports DAU and MAU. Its Q4 FY2025 letter shows daily active users rising from 40.5 million to 52.7 million year on year (company figures).                                                                                                                                                                                      | S24      |

### Duolingo: not verified from a first-party page in this pass

Gems as a virtual currency (referenced in S19 as funding XP boosts, but earning and spending rules not read), daily quests, XP rules, reward chest odds, hearts or energy, notification timing and copy, comeback and win-back flows, and its A/B-tested onboarding changes. Duolingo's engagement design is widely discussed by third parties, but those accounts are **commentary** and were not used as evidence here. Anything about these in the other documents is a **[H]** or **[K]**.

### Behavioural science used to reason about retention

| Idea                                        | Primary source                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Use in Dojo                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fogg Behavior Model                         | Behaviour occurs when motivation, ability and a prompt converge [S26].                                                                                                                                                     | Make the next action _easy_ (ability) and name it in the prompt, instead of raising motivation with pressure.                                                     |
| Self-determination theory                   | Support for autonomy, competence and relatedness fosters more volitional, higher-quality motivation and persistence, and contexts built on salient rewards or threats tend to produce controlled regulation instead [S27]. | A design test for every mechanism: does it support the learner's autonomy, show real competence, and connect them to others, or does it only reward and threaten? |
| Goal-gradient effect                        | Studied by Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng [S28]. **Full text was not read**; only the citation was confirmed.                                                                                                                  | Progress that is visible and near completion may speed effort. **[H]**, to be tested, and to be checked against the paper before being cited as a finding.        |
| Loss aversion, variable rewards, Hook model | Widely cited in product writing. **Not researched from primary sources here.** [K]                                                                                                                                         | Named only to say what Dojo avoids (see below). No recommendation depends on them.                                                                                |

### Aggressive retention mechanisms: what they are, and Dojo's stance

The regulator's staff report on manipulative design confirms that such techniques are a live regulatory concern [S29]. The report's categories were **not** read in full here, so the table reasons from first principles and the Dojo requirements, not from the report's taxonomy.

| Mechanism                                              | Why products use it                          | Risk                                                              | Dojo stance                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Daily streak that resets to zero                       | Loss aversion keeps people coming back [S16] | Anxiety, shame, and practising for the number instead of learning | **Adapt.** Weekly streak with built-in rest and no punitive reset (see strategy).                                                             |
| Purchasable streak protection                          | Monetises the fear of loss                   | Pay-to-avoid-loss; reads as coercive                              | **Avoid.** Protection is automatic and free.                                                                                                  |
| Demotion in a weekly league                            | Keeps competitive users active [S18]         | Loss framing; grind to avoid demotion                             | **Defer.** If ever built: opt-in, no demotion, matched cohorts, transparent rules.                                                            |
| Guilt-driven or time-pressured notifications           | Raise open and return rates                  | Coercion; erodes trust; fatigue. **[K]** Not verified here        | **Avoid.** Opt-in, user-chosen times, informational content only, easy mute.                                                                  |
| Variable-ratio reward chests                           | Surprise sustains behaviour                  | Compulsion loop; unrelated to learning (S19 shows a reward chest) | **Avoid for rewards that look like gambling.** Rewards recognise effort or progress and are predictable.                                      |
| Scarcity and fake urgency (countdowns, limited offers) | Drive conversion                             | Deceptive                                                         | **Avoid.** Real deadlines only (a contest ends when it ends).                                                                                 |
| Social pressure ("your friend is waiting")             | Accountability [S19]                         | Shame, obligation                                                 | **Adapt.** Opt-in accountability; nudges are optional and framed as encouragement; users can mute or leave any group.                         |
| Hidden or hard opt-out                                 | Keeps users in competitive loops             | Violates autonomy [S27]                                           | **Avoid.** Every competitive or social feature is opt-in or one-tap off, as Duolingo itself offers for leagues and Friends Quests [S18, S19]. |

## What the research cannot tell Dojo

1. **Whether any of these mechanics improve developer learning.** The sources report engagement or completion, not skill gain. Duolingo reports efficacy studies exist [S25] but those concern language learning.
2. **Whether Dojo's audience wants competition.** Established platforms prove demand among competitive programmers and job seekers. It says little about developers who want to improve how they direct coding agents, which is a newer group.
3. **What a good agent-workflow practice item looks like.** No researched product teaches this. This is Dojo's open ground and its main product risk.
4. **How cheating behaves in an LLM-assisted world.** A contest on workflow quality invites different cheating (for example tuning prompts to the scorer) than a contest on code correctness. This was not researched and is flagged as a risk in [`FUTURE_ARCHITECTURE.md`](FUTURE_ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Decisions and open questions from this research

- **[D]** Whether Dojo competes on _code_ (a judge), on _workflow_ (the existing differentiator), or on both, and in what order. The research shows the code-contest ecosystem is crowded and costly to match [S01, S04, S09, S12].
- **[D]** Whether Dojo adopts any Duolingo-style social mechanic at all in its first releases, or waits until there are enough users for cohorts to make sense.
- **Open:** verify the **[K]** items and read S28's paper before citing the goal-gradient effect as a finding; read the FTC report's categories before using it as a compliance checklist.
